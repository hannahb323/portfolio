HONEYCOMB
CREAMERY

A rebrand for a Cambridge ice cream shop that feels whimsical, bold and indulgent, and lets its inventive flavors speak for themselves.

Food & restaurant branding The Cooper Union · 2026

Role

Brand strategy & identity

Course

Food & Restaurant Branding with Douglas Riccardi, The Cooper Union

Timeline

4 weeks, April 2026

Deliverables

Positioning, identity concepts, and mockups of packaging, signage, menu, website and socials

The goal

Create an ice cream shop brand that feels whimsical, bold and indulgent while highlighting Honeycomb's handcrafted, locally sourced magic.

The shop

Honeycomb Creamery serves small-batch flavors made with local ingredients in Cambridge, MA. Even the sprinkles and honeycomb candy are made from scratch. The menu balances the nostalgic and the inventive, and there's a line out the door every day, even if a few flavors are crossed off by the time you reach the front.

Who it's for

Local families and students, drawn in by flavors and toppings they can't get anywhere else, and coming back for the childlike thrill that a new favorite might be one scoop away.

01 · Today's scoop

Where the brand was melting

A forgettable logo

A sterile interior that doesn't match what they serve

Unbranded cups

Creative flavor names, but no descriptions in the shop

Socials that are consistent but underwhelming

Nothing explains what honeycomb is, and there are no samples

The big idea · time to rotate

Let the inventive flavors speak for themselves, with branding that's indulgent, informative and fun.

05 · Online

Website & socials

In this mockup, the website lets you filter flavors as Classic, Seasonal, Vegan or Soft Serve, and an “open now” badge shows today's hours. On Instagram, concept highlights cover flavors of the day, what's in season, local sourcing and behind the scenes.

Toppings

Beyond the logo

Flavor stories

Social posts that break down flavors, toppings and cakes, and show how new flavors get brainstormed.

Local roots

Spotlights and events with the farms and businesses behind the ingredients.

On the road

An ice cream truck for colleges and local events.

Little extras

A punch card, laser-cut logo spoons and, please, a few samples.

What I took away

Looking back, I wish I’d focused more on building the brand as a whole rather than just the logo. There’s so much of a story to tell in a rebrand, especially for a local, handcrafted spot like this one. Having such a blank canvas opened up endless possibilities, and I really appreciated that freedom.
